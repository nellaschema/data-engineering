# Data Fundamentals – Day 2
**Lecturer:** Ms. Michelle Alarcon

## Data Analysis Workflow

1. Define the problem.
2. Gather relevant data.
3. Check data quality.
4. Resolve quality issues.
5. Prepare the data.
6. Analyze the data.
7. Interpret the results.

## Data Quality

> Bad data leads to bad decisions, which lead to bad outcomes.

Poor data quality can cost up to **10× more** than working with clean data.

### Data quality dimensions

- Accuracy
- Consistency
- Completeness
- Auditability
- Validity
- Uniqueness
- Timeliness

Notes:
- Dirty data often originates from the process owner or subject-matter expert.
- Accuracy means the value is correct.
- Consistency checks whether values remain the same across different sources and formats.

---

## Business Problem vs. Analytics Problem

### Business Problem

An organizational issue that the company wants to solve.

Examples:
- How can we optimize scheduling?
- How can we improve operational efficiency?
- How can we reduce employee stress while maintaining productivity?

### Analytics Problem

Questions that can be answered using data.

Examples:
- What time of day has the highest number of Grab bookings?
- What season has the fewest bookings?
- How do booking behaviors change throughout the year?

### Rule of Thumb

- If analytics tools disappeared, would the problem still exist?
  - **Yes → Business Problem**
  - **No → Analytics Problem**

---

## Problem Framing

A business problem should be agreed upon before performing analysis.

Proper framing helps:
- Prevent solving the wrong problem.
- Align expectations between business owners and analysts.
- Identify the correct data requirements.

### Analytics Process

1. Frame the business problem.
2. Frame the analytics problem.
3. Identify data requirements.
4. Select the method.
5. Build the model.
6. Deploy the model.
7. Manage the model lifecycle.

## Class Discussion

### Can business questions be solved without data?

My answer:

While it may be possible to come up with a solution without data, it is unlikely to be the most effective one because there is no evidence to support the decision.

It is like a blind person trying to reach a destination without a guide or walking stick—you might eventually get there, but it will be much more difficult and less reliable.

## Responsible Use of Data

Principles:
- Collect only the data that is needed.
- Use data only for its intended purpose.
- Obtain consent.
- Protect data privacy.
- Acknowledge data quality limitations.
- Avoid bias.
- Be transparent.
- Document methods, assumptions, and analysis.

## Identifying Relevant Data

Approach:
1. Understand the business.
2. Define the business problem.
3. Define analytics questions.
4. Identify relevant data domains.
5. Determine data elements and metrics.

### Example

**Business Problem**

How can the company reduce employee stress while improving productivity?

**Analytics Questions**
- Does working longer hours increase employee stress?
- How do shift schedules affect stress levels?
- Is the company understaffed during certain times of the year?
- Do employees feel adequately supported during peak workloads?

**Relevant Data Domains**

| Data Domain | Data Elements |
|--------------|---------------|
| Employee wellness survey | Self-reported stress score |
| HR attendance | Daily logins, work hours |
| Scheduling | Shift schedules |
| Workforce planning | Staffing levels, overtime |

---

## Key Takeaways

- Define the business problem before analyzing data.
- Analytics questions should directly support the business problem.
- High-quality data leads to better decisions.
- Responsible data use is part of good data practice.
- The business context should determine what data is needed.
