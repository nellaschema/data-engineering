### The Goal Was Not to Master the Tools

One thing Sir Myk emphasized was that the program was never mainly about teaching us specific tools.

We used Databricks as our laboratory.

The tools we used today may change or disappear. New tools will appear, and different companies will use different technologies.

The important part is knowing how to approach a problem:

```text
Understand the problem
        ↓
Frame the problem properly
        ↓
Identify what needs to be done
        ↓
Choose the appropriate tool
        ↓
Build the solution
        ↓
Validate the results
```

The skill is therefore not simply:

> "I know Databricks."

It is closer to:

> "I understand the problem well enough to figure out which tool I need to learn."

Sir Myk also emphasized:

> "Data engineering is easy to learn but hard to master."

We are not expected to become masters of SQL, Python, Databricks, Git, CI/CD, orchestration, or governance during this program. That was never the objective.

The objective was:

> "We wanted to teach you how to think like a data engineer."

## Personal Governance for AI-Augmented Data Engineers

Another major topic was how we use AI as data engineers.

We were asked:

> How did ChatGPT show up in your Databricks journey?

This made me reflect on how much AI can be involved in the development process, especially when learning unfamiliar tools or debugging code.

### AI as an Intellectual Sparring Partner

I think of using an LLM as an **intellectual sparring partner**.

Instead of configuring AI to simply agree with me or give me an answer, I use it to:

- Challenge my ideas
- Critique my approach
- Identify weaknesses
- Stress-test my assumptions

AI can participate in the thinking process, but we need to understand and take responsibility for the final result.

Ms. Carmi summarized this as:

> "AI can automate execution. It cannot automatically transfer accountability."

The professional responsibility remains with the person who deployed the pipeline.

### The 4 Gates of Using GPT

We were given four questions to consider when using GPT:

#### 1. Data

> Am I allowed to expose this?

Before giving information to an AI system, I need to consider whether the data can be shared.

#### 2. AI

> Is this tool/environment appropriate?

Even if I am allowed to use the data, I still need to consider whether the AI tool or environment is appropriate for the task.

#### 3. Output

> Can I verify what GPT produced?

Getting an answer from GPT does not automatically make the answer correct.

I need to be able to check the output.

#### 4. Accountability

> Can I explain and defend the final result?

Even if GPT helped produce the code or solution, I am still responsible for the final result that I use or deploy.

### AI Responsibilities

The responsibilities discussed included:

1. Data governance: What am I about to expose?
2. Data minimization

Questions to ask: Should I give it this particular kind of information? Can I validate what it produces?
