# Key Learnings

## 1. Data Preparation

Data preparation is the process of collecting, organizing, and preparing data before analysis. A well-defined workflow is essential because it ensures that data is handled consistently and efficiently throughout the analytics process.

### Data Requirements

Before collecting data, start with the business problem and translate it into questions.

The recommended process is:

1. Define the problem.
2. Turn the problem into questions that need to be answered.
3. For each question, identify the data needed.
4. Group similar data into data domains.
5. List the necessary data elements.
6. Identify possible metrics and measures.
7. Review the lists for redundant or overlapping data.
8. Merge similar data domains.
9. Finalize the questions and required data.

### Entity Diagrams

We created an entity diagram using a sari-sari store example.

**Key takeaway:** Good tracking enables better decision-making. If the necessary information is available and properly organized, answering business questions becomes much easier.

## 2. Data Quality

Data quality determines how useful and reliable a dataset is.

### Common Issues

* Missing values
* Incorrect entries
* Duplicate records
* Inconsistent formats
* Invalid data

### Best Practices

* Consult the subject matter expert (SME) or process owner.
* Always make a copy of the original data before making changes.
* Validate cleaned data with the process owner.
* Document every finding and decision.

> **"Number one thing na hindi narerealize ng mga tao is the importance of documentation skills. Always document your findings."** — Sir Myk

> **"Data quality is not a one-time project. It must be continuous."**

We also discussed the laundry lifecycle as an example of how data moves through a process and where quality issues can occur.

### Group Activity

* Identify data quality issues

### Important Insight

Sometimes the business owner does not use advanced tools like Excel.

Even if a recommendation is technically correct, it may not be practical if it doesn't fit the existing workflow. Solutions should consider the needs and capabilities of the people who will actually use them.

## 3. Data Analysis

### Data Preparation vs. Data Transformation

**Data Preparation**

* Cleaning
* Organizing
* Validating
* Structuring data before analysis

**Data Transformation**

* Converting data into another format
* Aggregating
* Encoding
* Reshaping
* Feature engineering

### Personal Reflection

Because of my work in a biochemical genetics laboratory, I realized I already have extensive experience with **data preparation** through laboratory data validation, quality checks, and documentation.

However, I have very limited experience with **data transformation**, which is an area I want to strengthen throughout this program.

## 4. Types of Data Analysis

The four major types of data analysis are:

* **Descriptive** — What happened?
* **Diagnostic** — Why did it happen?
* **Predictive** — What is likely to happen?
* **Prescriptive** — What should we do?

We also discussed:

* Detecting anomalies
* Applying the Pareto Principle (80/20 rule)

## Exercise 2: Customer Loyalty Case Study

### Business Problem

Increase customer loyalty and increase transaction value.

### Questions to Explore

To better understand loyal customers:

* Who are they?
* What characteristics do they share?
* What products do they usually buy?
* When do they buy?
* Where do they buy?
* How much do they spend?

Next, compare loyal customers with non-loyal customers:

* Do some non-loyal customers have similar traits?
* Do they purchase similar products?
* Why do their purchasing behaviors differ?
* Can those customers be encouraged to become loyal customers?
* Are there customers with similar buying habits but different demographics?
* Can marketing strategies target those groups?

The objective is to combine all findings into actionable business recommendations.
